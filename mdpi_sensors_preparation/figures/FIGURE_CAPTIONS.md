**Figure 1.** Electronic architecture of the reference-monitored two-point active self-test (schematic). During the AFE test window, MUX 1 (S1/S2) replaces the sensor with the two-level AFE reference, so the response passes through the AFE and the ADC. During the ADC test window, MUX 2 (S3/S4) applies the two-level ADC reference directly to the ADC input, so the response depends on the ADC alone. The ADC transfer estimated in the second window is removed from the record before the AFE transfer is estimated from the first. A buffered monitor channel (S5) measures the reference levels that are actually applied, with a gain error bounded by ±E. The digital back-end computes settled-plateau calibration values and phase-robust transient descriptors and classifies them with a 20–96–48 multilayer perceptron into the faulty layer (four classes) and the fault class (nine classes). Stars mark the locations of the simulated faults.

**Figure 2.** Timing of the active self-test. **Figure 3.** Processing and evaluation pipeline (schematic). The behavioral model and the ngspice netlists generate records with injected faults (9 classes), component tolerances, noise and 12-bit quantization. The two-point reference test is applied at five edge phases ψ, and the reference monitor reports the reference levels with a bounded gain error E. Twenty descriptors are extracted from each record and classified by the 20–96–48 multilayer perceptron. Performance is summarized by macro-F1, compared with the acceptance criteria A–G of the frozen protocol, and its uncertainty is estimated with a 5000-resample paired cluster bootstrap over operating conditions.

**Figure 4.** Fault signatures in descriptor space (illustrative; behavioral model, development seed 20261097, 60 records per class, not used for any decision). Box plots show the median, the interquartile range and whiskers at 1.5 times the interquartile range (outliers omitted) of six of the 20 descriptors for the nine classes, colored by faulty layer. (a) ADC gain and (b) ADC offset, estimated from the ADC-reference window, respond to ADC gain and ADC offset faults and stay flat for sensor and AFE faults. (c) AFE gain and (d) AFE offset, estimated from the AFE-reference window after ADC calibration, respond to AFE faults; a bandwidth fault lowers the apparent gain and raises the apparent offset because the plateaus no longer settle. (e) The fitted time constant τ/P isolates AFE bandwidth faults. (f) High-frequency noise in the sensor intervals isolates sensor-noise faults. None of the six descriptors separates sensor bias or sensor gain faults from the healthy class.

**Figure 5.** Sensitivity to the sampling phase (simulation).
