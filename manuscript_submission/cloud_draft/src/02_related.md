# Related Work and Research Gap <<sec:related>>

## Sensor Validation and Sensor Fault Diagnosis <<sec:rel_sensor>>

The self-validating sensor proposed by Henry and Clarke combines the measurement with internal diagnostics and delivers the on-line uncertainty of each value [@henry1993], and the technology that followed from this concept is reviewed in [@feng2007]. In parallel, fault diagnosis methods use analytical models, signal features or learned classifiers to detect and isolate faulty sensors within a system [@isermann2006; @li2020]. Balaban et al. model bias, drift, scaling and dropout faults and address the separation of sensor faults from faults of the monitored system [@balaban2009]. In all of these approaches the sensor channel is observed through its output. The interface electronics are treated as part of the sensor, and a fault of the amplifier or of the converter is not distinguished from a fault of the sensing element unless a known stimulus is available.

## Built-In Self-Test of Sensors and Mixed-Signal Circuits <<sec:rel_bist>>

Built-in self-test (BIST) supplies such a stimulus. For micro-electromechanical sensors, the built-in self-test of the transducer has been studied extensively [@hantos2021; @mir2006; @deb2006], and sensor front-ends with self-monitoring, self-trimming and self-healing functions have been reviewed by Alraho et al. [@alraho2022]. For analog and mixed-signal integrated circuits, test access is standardized by the mixed-signal test bus [@ieee1149_4], and recent BIST schemes check invariant signals inside the circuit [@pavlidis2021] or are oriented toward defects and assessed by defect simulation [@ganji2024; @sunter2016]. These techniques are designed into an integrated circuit and aim at detecting defects of the device, in production or in the field. They do not aim at telling the user of an assembled sensor node whether the sensing element, the front-end or the converter has degraded.

The testing of ADCs follows standardized terminology and test methods [@ieee1241]. A point that is directly relevant here is the quality of the stimulus. Jin et al. showed that the linearity of an ADC can be tested with a signal source that is less linear than the converter, provided that the stimulus error is identified and removed [@jin2005], and Chen et al. carried this principle into an ADC BIST with a low-accuracy on-chip stimulus [@chen2018]. The present work shares the premise that the stimulus cannot be assumed exact. It differs in the remedy: the reference levels are measured by a separate low-speed channel, because the quantities of interest are the static gain and offset of two cascaded stages, for which a scaling of the stimulus cannot be separated from a scaling of the converter gain by redundancy in the stimulus alone ({{S:monitor}}).

Commercial converters for sensor signal chains embed diagnostics that are close in spirit to a reference test, such as reference detection, internal test voltages and excitation currents for open- and short-circuit detection [@mccarthy2022; @adi_an1320]. They flag specific conditions of the device and its connections. The documentation that we consulted does not describe a procedure that attributes a parametric deviation to the sensor, the front-end or the converter.

## Self-Test of Analog Parts in Microcontroller Systems <<sec:rel_czaja>>

The closest prior work that we found is the series of self-test methods for analog parts terminated by an ADC in microcontroller systems by Czaja [@czaja2013; @czaja2016]. In these methods the microcontroller applies a square pulse to the analog part, samples the time response with the ADC, and maps the samples to a point in a measurement space in which precomputed localization curves identify the faulty component. The 2013 article reports an experimental verification [@czaja2013]. The 2016 article describes an implementation in which a 2 ms pulse and 64 samples are used for a Sallen–Key low-pass filter and the conversions are triggered by the same timer that generates the pulse [@czaja2016].

The comparison with these methods is instructive in both directions. They localize single soft faults of individual passive components, which is a finer resolution inside the analog part than the layer-level localization studied here, and one of them has been verified on hardware, which the present work has not. On the other hand, in the implementation described in [@czaja2016] the stimulus amplitude is the supply voltage and the sampling instants are locked to the stimulus, and in the texts that we could read (the abstract of [@czaja2013] and the full text of [@czaja2016]) faults of the ADC or of a sensor are not considered, nor is a stimulus edge that is asynchronous to the sampling clock. The present work addresses exactly these three points: a second reference path that isolates the ADC, a monitor that removes the assumption of a known stimulus level, and descriptors that tolerate an arbitrary edge phase. It should therefore be read as complementary to component-level self-test, not as a replacement for it.

## Data-Driven Fault Diagnosis of Analog Circuits <<sec:rel_ml>>

Learned classifiers have been applied to the diagnosis of analog circuits since the work of Aminian and Aminian, who classified wavelet features of the circuit response with a neural network [@aminian2000]; the field has been surveyed in [@binu2017]. Recent studies replace hand-crafted features by deep networks that operate on the raw response or on time–frequency representations, including deep belief networks [@zhao2018], one-dimensional convolutional networks [@yang2020; @zhang2021; @gao2024], multi-scale convolutional networks [@yang2023] and autoencoders [@shang2024]. Machine learning in the testing of integrated circuits more broadly is reviewed in [@afacan2021; @roy2021; @stratigopoulos2018].

Three features of this literature matter for the present work. First, the circuit under test is typically a single filter stage, such as a Sallen–Key or a four-operational-amplifier biquad filter, and the classes are faults of its components [@zhang2021; @gao2024; @shang2024]. Second, the data are in most cases generated by simulation [@yang2020; @gao2024], although a verification on an actual circuit is also reported [@shang2024]. Third, the reported accuracies are high, for example 98.93% in [@gao2024]. Such figures are not comparable with the results reported here. They refer to a different task, and, to the extent that we could verify from the abstracts and the full texts available to us, the evaluations do not include an uncertain stimulus level, an uncontrolled sampling instant or test conditions grouped by operating condition. The sensitivity of learned diagnosis to differences between training and deployment data is a recognized problem: Zhang et al. apply domain adaptation to the diagnosis of an amplifier circuit under varying external and internal conditions [@zhang2024], and transfer learning for fault diagnosis is surveyed in [@liw2022].

## Estimation of Transient Parameters from Sampled Step Responses <<sec:rel_transient>>

The terms that describe transitions and pulses, and the methods for measuring them, are standardized [@ieee181]. The identification of a first-order model with a time delay from a sampled step response is a classical problem [@bi1999], and a delay that is a fraction of the sampling period can be estimated from sampled data [@abdalmoaty2022]. When the model is linear in some of its parameters, these can be eliminated in closed form and the search carried out over the remaining nonlinear parameters, a technique known as variable projection [@golub1973; @golub2003; @oleary2013]. The descriptors of {{S:transient}} use these established tools. Training a classifier on data in which a nuisance property of the simulator is randomized, so that the classifier becomes insensitive to it, is likewise an established idea [@tobin2017]; here the randomized property is the edge phase.

## Research Gap <<sec:gap>>

Within the literature that we searched, we did not find a study that localizes faults at the level of the sensor, the front-end and the converter of a sensor interface while treating both the uncertainty of the reference levels and the sampling phase of the reference edges as test conditions. Self-validating sensors and sensor fault diagnosis observe the chain through its output. BIST for integrated circuits targets device defects. Component-level self-test in microcontroller systems assumes a known stimulus, a fault-free converter and synchronous sampling. Data-driven analog fault diagnosis reports high accuracy on single filter stages with a known stimulus. This statement describes the result of a targeted, non-exhaustive search and is not a claim of priority.

What the present study adds is therefore limited and specific: an analysis and a controlled simulation experiment showing how much of the localization performance of a two-point reference test depends on monitoring the reference and on describing the transient in a phase-independent way, and how much does not.
